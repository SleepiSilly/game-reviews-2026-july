Axolot Games Spent 6 Years Making an Update and Served Us Spaghetti Code: My Scrap Mechanic 1.0 "Drilling Thunder" Review

Dev_In_Training_02 July 2026 // v1.0.0

After ten years of Early Access, Axolot Games finally pushed to main and released Scrap Mechanic 1.0 with the long-awaited Chapter 2 "Drilling Thunder" update. The hype across the community was pretty significant, with lots of people being excited for the release.

However, as someone who spends time reading technical documentation, building complex logic controllers, and preparing for a career in software architecture, I am already very underwhelmed.

We waited six full years for Chapter 2, and what we got is a textbook case of scope creep, unoptimized technical debt, and bloated feature sets.

01. Storyline & Voice-Acted NPCs

They tried to turn Survival mode into a full narrative campaign. You crash land, investigate the reason the Farmbots went hostile, follow questlines with voice-acted NPCs at locations like the Farmers Hideout, and try to escape the planet.

While the voice acting is pretty good, bolting a linear narrative onto a sandbox game just feels weird. The engineering resources spent on dialogue and narrative triggers would have been better utilized refactoring the physics thread. I came here to build 16-cylinder engine piston cars, not engage in scripted conversations to unlock rewards.

02. Excavation Island & The Underground

The Excavation Island was shown off as a major feature—a large zone with deep cave systems where you use the new Plasma Drill and Plasma Saw to mine stronger minerals. On paper, is sounds like a good addition. In execution, it's a big performance issue.

The subsurface raycasting and dynamic cave lighting negatively impact GPU performance. When combined with the new surface biomes and weather systems, it's clear their profiling tools were overlooked. Instead of multithreading the terrain rendering, the frame rate gets absolutely FRIED whenever you enter a deep cave.

03. Claygun & Voxel Deforming

The Claygun basically lets you make and sculpt your own terrain in both Creative and Survival mode. While it's a really interesting concept, dynamic voxel destruction becomes demanding on physics calculations.

Furthermore, bots can now navigate through player-created barriers. This creates a conflict when the A* pathfinding node graph fails as a Minerbot attempts to tunnel through player-placed clay. Having multiple bots enter a pathfinding loop, driving CPU usage to 100%, is not in any way good game design.

04. Bot Roster Overhaul & Combat Balancing

The enemy roster practically tripled with Cablebots, Minerbots, Seekerbots, specialized Totebot and Tapebot variants, and the Trash Bot and Drillbot bossfights. To counter them, Axolot added new weapons such as the Scrap Spud Gun, Cornade, and the Fire Launcher.

However, Axolot's approach to game balance involves giving the Red Explosive Totebot massive Area of Effect (AOE) damage that instantly destroys complex structural builds. This is NOT engaging combat; it's just artificial difficulty resulting from bad enemy balancing. If a game forces players to use meta weapons and materials simply to survive a single unit, then the core design is completely flawed.

05. Blueprints, Scrap City Garage & New Parts (The Only Good PR)

Credit is due for the new schematic system and the ability to spawn Creative mode builds directly into Survival via the Scrap City Garage; this is effective UX design.

The new building components—ladders, UV grow beds, digital clocks, freezers, and interactive blocks—add functional modularity. However, it is fair to ask why implementing a blueprint loader for Survival required ten years. Implementing JSON serialization for blueprint loading is a straightforward task that should not have taken this long.

06. Technical Netcode "Fixes" & The Post-Launch Patch Parade

Axolot claimed major netcode and multiplayer performance optimizations. While there are slight improvements compared to 2021, multi-joint complex vehicles remain prone to physics desynchronization when a second player joins a host session.

Furthermore, the necessity of pushing patches 1.0.1 and 1.0.2 within 72 hours of release to address quest progression, chest logic errors, and UI glitches indicates that the QA testing phase was rushed to meet a deadline.

Corporate Software Engineer Verdict

5.5 / 10

Scrap Mechanic 1.0 is playable, but as someone who prioritizes code optimization, clean system architecture, and realistic product roadmaps, this release is disappointing. Axolot Games spent six years layering features without resolving the underlying legacy technical debt. Had this been my sprint backlog, these gimmicks would have been deprioritized in favor of physics multithreading and native modding API development.
